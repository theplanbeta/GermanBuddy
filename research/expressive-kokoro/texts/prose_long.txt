Most cities were not designed for the weather they now face. Drains that were sized for the storms of the last century overflow several times a year, and streets that once stayed dry now flood within minutes. The problem is not only the amount of rain, but how quickly it arrives. Engineers have started to respond in two ways. The first is to build bigger pipes, which is expensive, slow, and disruptive, because every street has to be dug up. The second is to let the city itself absorb more water. Parks are lowered slightly so that they can hold a temporary lake, car parks are paved with porous stone, and flat roofs are planted with grass. Neither approach is enough on its own. Pipes fail when they are blocked, and green spaces fill up during long storms. But together, they buy time. And in a flood, a few extra minutes can decide whether water reaches people's homes.
