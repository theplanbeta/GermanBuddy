At precisely 7:43 a.m. on Wednesday, November 12th, Dr. Müller received an unexpected phone call. “Wait… WHAT?!” she exclaimed, her voice trembling. “You're telling me that 37.8% of the €2.45 million budget has simply… disappeared?” There was a long, uncomfortable silence. Then she whispered, almost to herself, “Das kann doch nicht wahr sein.” Outside, the rain was falling gently on the streets of Zürich, while a French tourist struggled to pronounce Eichhörnchen. Meanwhile, Professor O'Sullivan, a 62-year-old neuroscientist, was explaining the difference between Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis to an audience of 1,250 people. “Consider this,” he said, deliberately slowing his speech. “A patient receives 0.75 milligrams of medication every six hours. But what happens if the dosage increases by 15%?” Suddenly, someone shouted from the back: “¡Cuidado! Achtung! Watch out!” A glass shattered. Everyone froze. Three seconds passed. Then five. Finally, a little girl burst into laughter and said, “Relax, everyone! It was just my cat.” The professor sighed, chuckled softly, and replied, “Well… that was unexpected, wasn't it?” And somewhere in the distance, a clock struck midnight—once, twice, three times—although nobody could explain why.
