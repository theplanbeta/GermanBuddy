HEARSHOT DOCUMENT READER - WHAT TO LOOK AT

Each file 1-5 shows the same pages twice, side by side:
left  = plain PDF text, the way most reader apps get it;
right = what Hearshot would read aloud with the default settings.
The first line of each file says what to look for.

WHERE IT STILL FAILS (honest list, from the measurements)
- Scientific papers with many tables, formulas and figure labels: some table notes and labels
  are still read, and a few body lines get skipped as if they were captions (scores 68-82 of 100).
- Exam sheets and worksheets with answer boxes, multiple-choice grids and maths: up to a quarter
  of the text is lost or read out of order (scores 59-92).
- Magazine layouts: a subtitle that runs across two columns can come out jumbled, and boxed
  sidebars ("At a glance", "Did you know?") are read in the middle of the story.
- Slide decks: the words are right, but bullet points are often merged into one long paragraph.
- Newspaper pages with several stories: stories that jump between columns are sometimes joined
  in the wrong order; the app shows a "reading order may be wrong" warning on such pages, but the
  warning also appears on some pages that are fine.
- Long dashes become short hyphens ("writing is-how difficult" instead of "writing is - how"),
  so the voice may run those words together.
- Scanned pages need a separate "recognise text" step (a few seconds per page on a computer,
  slower on a phone). Very blurry or low-resolution scans can still come out with errors.
- Formulas are not read; they are replaced by the word "Equation." Tables are replaced by
  "Table omitted." (a setting can turn table reading on).
- German documents: works, but compound words split over two lines are rejoined using the
  document itself only; no German dictionary yet.
- Not yet tried on the phone at all.
